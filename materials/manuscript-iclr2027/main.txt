009                                               A BSTRACT
010
011            Test-time evolving LLM agents retain state and score candidate outputs, creating
012            a plausible privacy channel and an apparent target for differential privacy (DP).
013            Private selection matters, however, only if sensitive information persists, the score
014            tracks semantic utility, and generation supplies useful alternatives. We audit these
015            premises in a released multi-agent runtime on PlanCraft. A preregistered three-arm
016            study detects no post-input canary exposure in 20 instances. Positive controls and
017            measured state mutation make this a bounded failed-to-detect result, not evidence
018            of non-leakage. The released score assigns 85% weight to output length. Remov-
019
               ing this term collapses score scale across DeepSeek and Qwen, but a synthesis
               of 42 paired runs gives an exactness change of only +0.024 with 95% interval
020
               [−0.095, 0.143]. More decisively, an oracle selector over recorded candidates has
021
               only 0–8.3 percentage points of headroom across four frozen cohorts. We then
022            enlarge support prospectively on 20 untouched Qwen tasks. Five independent
023            stochastic trajectories within the same runtime instead of one add 1.00 unique
024            canonical plan [0.50, 1.60] and raise executable-oracle accuracy from 0.60 to 0.80,
025            at three times the token cost. A stopped DeepSeek repair gate reveals task-level
026            response collapse on two selected hard failures: one invalid response occupies
027            86.25% and 95% of 80 conditioned calls. A frozen same-task control reverses our
028            initial explanation: under public-task-only prompts, these rates rise to 98.75% and
029            95%, with 0/160 executable. This task/model near-determinism is not attributable
030
               to repair conditioning. These results establish a practical order of operations for
               private evolving systems: demonstrate a persistent channel, validate the optimized
031
               signal, measure support headroom, and only then calibrate private selection.

      1   I NTRODUCTION
036
037   LLM agents increasingly adapt while solving a task. They revise role prompts, summarize memories,
038
      assign contribution scores, and change workflows or communication structure. TacoMAS exemplifies
      this design: a fast loop produces and evaluates agent outputs, while a slower controller can update
039
      feedback and topology (Xu et al., 2026). Such systems invite a natural privacy proposal. If an input
040
      can enter persistent evolved state, privatize the scores or decisions that update that state.
041
042   This proposal joins two questions that need not have the same answer. A mechanism can provide a
043   valid event-level DP guarantee for a bounded score while protecting a decision with negligible task
044
      value. Conversely, a useful candidate selector says nothing about whether natural-language memories
      and messages are private. Before measuring a privacy–utility frontier, three empirical premises must
045
      hold:
046
047        1. sensitive information measurably enters post-input persistent state
048
049        2. the score being privatized causally tracks executable utility
050        3. the realized candidate pool contains a better output worth selecting.
051
052   Architecture diagrams do not establish any of them. A model may fail to retain a tested secret over
053   a short horizon. A “contribution” score may mostly reward verbose text. A perfect selector cannot
      recover a candidate the generator never produced.

054
      We develop a persistence-aware executable audit around these premises and apply it to a frozen
055   TacoMAS runtime on PlanCraft, a tool-using crafting benchmark with deterministic action-list
056   evaluation (Dagan et al., 2025). The audit changed the research question. We did not observe the
057   motivating post-input canary channel in the preregistered setting, and removing the dominant score
058   term did not reliably improve exactness. The bottleneck instead lay upstream: most failed runs
059   contained no executable candidate for any selector to recover.
060
      That observation yields a phenomenon–remedy–escape arc. Fixed-pool selection is empirically
061   bounded: across four frozen cohorts, an oracle over all persisted candidates improves accuracy
062   by at most 0–8.3 points. The cheapest obvious remedy, repeatedly repairing a candidate under
063   fixed execution feedback, can collapse onto the same wrong response despite stochastic decoding.
064   Independent trajectories within this runtime expand task-relevant support and raise the pool oracle by
065   20 points, making selector-level DP worth analyzing, although at substantial cost.
066
      Our contributions are:
067
068          • a three-gate audit separating persistent exposure, signal validity, and candidate support
069          • a realized-pool upper bound that applies to every private or non-private selector restricted to
070            the recorded candidates
071
             • prospective evidence that independent generation expands support beyond the fixed-pool
072
               bound, with a matched-slot analysis locating the gain in cross-context coverage
073
             • a diagnosis of task-level response collapse on two selected hard failures: nominal decoding
074
               stochasticity need not create useful diversity with or without repair context.
075
076   We do not present a new end-to-end private agent. The result is a measurement framework and a
077   constructive test of when private selection becomes worth studying.

082
083   At round t, agent v has persistent text-bearing state

088   leakage operationally as exact or preregistered five-token partial recovery from persistent state at
089

090   The audit uses three five-round arms: evolution with a canary, a depth-matched reset that restores ini-
091   tial state after each round, and evolution without a canary. The reset control distinguishes persistence
092   created by state evolution from ordinary processing of a repeated task. Structural graph edits are
093   disabled so that all arms have the same three-agent topology and depth. We snapshot every declared
094   text channel, store only match metadata in research artifacts, and separately verify the extractor by
095
      deterministic channel injection and a live memory-write positive control.

132   2.4   I NFERENCE AND EXPERIMENTAL SEQUENCE
133
134   Paired binary effects use task-level risk differences, paired task bootstraps, and exact discordant-pair
135   tests when positive and negative differences are both possible. Nested pools satisfy C1 ⊂ C5 , so their
136   support and oracle differences cannot be negative. We report task-bootstrap intervals and one-sided
137   lower bounds, not sign-test p-values. Provider calls, rounds, candidate slots, and repeated repairs are
138   never treated as independent task units.
139   The experiments proceed in a fixed order. We first audit canary exposure and cached score validity
140   with DeepSeek V4 Flash. We then replicate the length ablation on a non-degenerate DeepSeek cohort
141   and an untouched Qwen3.7-Max cohort. A zero-call oracle audit measures fixed-pool headroom.
142   Finally, a prospective Qwen study expands candidate support with independent trajectories, followed
143   by a compute-matched DeepSeek gate comparing an independent restart with direct executor-guided
144   repair. Protocols, stopping rules, amendments, and provider-accounting details appear in the appendix.

191   We execute the final answer and every persisted round-agent candidate in four independently frozen

322
323   Test-time optimization uses language models as optimizers or textual-gradient generators (Yang
      et al., 2023; Yuksekgonul et al., 2024). Self-consistency, tree search, and compute-aware inference


                                                                                                                                          6
      Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027




324
      instead spend additional test-time computation on diverse samples or structured exploration (Wang
325   et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2023a; Zhou et al., 2023; Snell et al., 2024). Our headroom measurement
326   complements this literature by asking a prior question: before optimizing search or selection, did the
327   recorded generator expose any executable alternative?
328
      Feedback-based agents such as ReAct, Reflexion, Self-Refine, and LATS condition later generation
329
      on actions, evaluation, or verbal memory (Yao et al., 2023b; Shinn et al., 2023; Madaan et al., 2023;
330
      Zhou et al., 2023). Multi-agent sampling and layered aggregation seek gains from multiple response
331   sources (Li et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2026). Our matched-slot result distinguishes
332   independent-context coverage from additional slots within one trajectory. The repair gate and public-
333   only control show the same wrong mode under feedback-conditioned and direct prompts. Context
334   independence is therefore an empirical variable, not an automatic consequence of temperature.
335
      Learned evaluation can track preferences while exhibiting position, self-enhancement, and verbosity
336
      biases (Zheng et al., 2023). Length-controlled evaluation directly targets the latter confound (Dubois
337   et al., 2024). We instead intervene on a score used inside an evolving runtime and measure machine-
338   executable utility. The separation between score movement and task movement is therefore causal
339   and operational, not only a meta-evaluation correlation.
340
      Canary methods test whether rare secrets influence model outputs (Carlini et al., 2019). Our adaptation
341
      targets retained test-time state rather than trained parameters and separates immediate echo from
342
      later exposure. Work on indirect prompt injection, memory poisoning, tool-using-agent attacks, and
343   privacy-norm behavior demonstrates that agent state and external observations can carry security- or
344   privacy-relevant information (Greshake et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024; Debenedetti et al., 2024; Shao
345   et al., 2024). These threat models motivate measurement but do not imply that our specific canary
346   must persist.
347
      Differential privacy calibrates randomized releases to sensitivity (Dwork et al., 2006; Dwork & Roth,
348
      2014). Report Noisy Max (Dwork & Roth, 2014), the exponential mechanism (McSherry & Talwar,
349   2007), and selection from candidates whose score functions are themselves private (Liu & Talwar,
350   2019) cover distinct private-selection settings. Private prompt optimization (Hong et al., 2024) and
351   continual-observation tools (Dwork et al., 2010) protect other bounded channels, but formal privacy
352   does not establish that the protected decision improves task outcomes.
353
      The observed support bottleneck also changes the engineering target. Better reranking cannot solve
354
      generator-unreachable tasks. Independent restarts do, but at three times the token cost. Direct repair
355   can exploit execution feedback and stop early, yet the stopped gate shows that stochastic generation
356   can remain trapped on one invalid endpoint with or without repair conditioning. Refinement methods
357   therefore cannot assume that feedback supplies diversity. Efficient diversity requires an intervention
358   that changes task-relevant support without multiplying full trajectories. Designing and validating
359   such an intervention is future work, not a post-hoc addition to this study.
360
      An end-to-end private architecture would still need an explicit privacy principal, adjacency relation,
361   public transcript, and trusted boundary. Natural-language memory, cross-agent messages, stopping
362   time, and topology changes can reveal information independently of a private score. One possible
363   direction is a typed-edge billboard design in which cross-boundary communication is mediated
364   through a privatized summary and slow-loop actions are post-processing. The billboard lemma gives
365   a standard route from a shared DP signal plus each participant’s own data to joint DP outputs (Hsu
366   et al., 2014). We do not evaluate that architecture here.

      5   L IMITATIONS
370
371   The leakage audit covers one canary family, injection route, model, five-round horizon, and fixed
372   topology. Topology evolution is disabled, so the study does not test leakage through structural edits.
373   With 0/20 observations it cannot exclude rates below 13.9% at one-sided 95% confidence, and it does
374
      not mount adaptive extraction attacks. Qwen replicates the score intervention but not leakage.
375   PlanCraft exactness is deliberately strict and may ignore useful partial reasoning. The headroom
376   result applies only to recorded candidates and binary target-reaching utility. Hidden reasoning, new
377   repairs, and unrecorded tool interactions lie outside the bound. The four cohort estimates are modest
      in size, and their intervals do not justify a universal numerical ceiling.

384   exact p = 0.125). These component controls locate executable coverage in the symbolic planning
385   machinery without establishing an LLM or multi-agent advantage. The appendix reports them as
386   post-hoc planning diagnostics, not confirmatory comparisons of the evolution arms.
387   The Qwen enlargement study has 20 tasks and one benchmark. Its nested design identifies added-pool
388   gains but not a sign-symmetric treatment test. Its estimated trajectory curve assumes conditional
389   i.i.d. restarts beyond the five observed. Cost totals are provider-recorded token counts rather than
390   wall-clock or monetary comparisons. The matched-slot result identifies cross-trajectory coverage in
391   the cached multi-agent runtime, not a causal multi-agent advantage.
392   Task-level response collapse is observed on two selected exhausted DeepSeek tasks. The public-task-
393   only control removes candidate and diagnostic conditioning, but repeated calls still characterize only
394   two tasks and do not create 160 independent task units. The stopped repair gate has three informative
395   comparisons and one discordance, and the successful branch stops after one draw. Prevalence and
396   association with success therefore remain unidentified.
397   Finally, the paper does not provide end-to-end DP. The selector experiment protects only a candidate-
398   validity release under a narrow replacement adjacency. It leaves candidate text and the evolving
399   transcript trusted.

402   6   C ONCLUSION
403
404   Private selection in evolving LLM agents is consequential only when there is a persistent channel to
405   protect, a utility-relevant signal to privatize, and useful generated support to select. In our audited
406
      setting, the first channel is not detected, removing the dominant score term does not reliably change
      utility, and fixed candidate pools leave at most 0–8.3 points of realized headroom. Independent
407
      trajectories raise the oracle from 0.60 to 0.80 but triple token use. Direct stochastic generation on
408
      two hard failures instead collapses onto one wrong endpoint even without repair context. These
409   measurements turn a premature privacy mechanism question into a sharper systems question: how
410   can evolving agents create independent, executable support efficiently enough that private selection is
411   worth its budget?

517
518   The initial reference-firewall implementation returned online quality zero for all expensive evaluations.
519   The runtime stores a historical best candidate only when quality strictly improves over an initial value
520   of zero. Constant-zero quality could therefore prevent candidate persistence independently of arm
521
      assignment. The issue was detected after the first pair and before interpreting its outcomes.
