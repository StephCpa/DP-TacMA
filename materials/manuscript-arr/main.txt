Before Candidate Selection in Test-Time LLM Agents: A Scope-Limited
Audit of Exposure, Score Interventions, and Realized Candidate Support

Anonymous ACL submission

001

Abstract

002

Test-time LLM agents retain natural-language
state, score intermediate responses, and revise their outputs while solving tasks. Before adding a candidate selector or a privacy
mechanism, one should check three preconditions: whether protected content enters persistent state, whether changing the score has
downstream consequences, and whether the
generator exposes executable alternatives worth
selecting. We apply this audit order to one
configuration of a released multi-agent runtime, TacoMAS with a fixed three-agent topology and structural edits disabled, on the PlanCraft benchmark. In a three-arm canary study
with 120 instances per arm (360 runs), no exact or five-token partial canary was recovered
from post-input persistent state under our canary family, injection route, observer, and fiveround horizon (evolve-arm one-sided 95% upper bound 2.5%). Removing the fast-round
length term, which carries 85% of the heuristic
score weight, sharply lowers recorded scores
for DeepSeek and Qwen, but exactness effects
in the primary cohorts are heterogeneous and
inconclusive. In four frozen cohorts, 43 of
46 failed runs contain no parser-accepted executable candidate in the recorded pool, so selectors restricted to that pool could have gained
only 0–8.3 percentage points. Adding four independent stochastic restarts raises the offline
executable oracle from 0.60 to 0.80 at roughly
three times the token cost. These results scope
where selection and privacy mechanisms could
matter in this runtime; they do not provide endto-end privacy or a general prevalence estimate.

Introduction

Language-model agents increasingly adapt while
solving a task. They revise role prompts, summarize natural-language memories, assign contribution scores, and change workflows or communication structure. TacoMAS exemplifies this design:
a fast loop produces and evaluates agent outputs,
1

while a slower controller can update feedback and
topology (Xu et al., 2026). Such systems invite
a natural privacy proposal. If an input can enter
persistent state, privatize the scores or decisions
that update that state.
This proposal joins two questions that need not
have the same answer. A mechanism can provide
a valid event-level DP guarantee for a bounded
score while protecting a decision with negligible
task value. Conversely, a useful candidate selector
says nothing about whether natural-language memories and messages are private. Before measuring
a privacy–utility frontier, three empirical premises
must hold:

044

1. protected information measurably enters postinput persistent state

058

2. changing the score to be privatized has a detectable consequence for executable utility

060

3. the realized candidate pool contains a better
output worth selecting.

062

Architecture diagrams do not establish any of them.
A model may fail to retain a tested secret over a
short horizon. A “contribution” score may mostly
reward verbose text. A perfect selector cannot recover a candidate the generator never produced.
We organize these checks into a scope-limited
audit and apply it to one configuration of TacoMAS on PlanCraft, a tool-using crafting benchmark with deterministic action-list evaluation (Dagan et al., 2025). Structural birth/death and edge
edits are disabled in every study, so all runs share
a fixed three-agent topology; the results describe
this fixed-topology slice of the runtime, not topology evolution. In this configuration we did not
detect the motivating post-input canary channel,
removing the dominant score term did not reliably
change exactness, and most failed runs contained
no parser-accepted executable candidate that a selector restricted to the recorded pool could recover.

2.4

Inference and experimental sequence

218

Paired binary effects use task-level risk differences,
paired task bootstraps, and exact discordant-pair
tests when positive and negative differences are
both possible. When a small-sample bootstrap is
degenerate, we flag it and give an exact bound.
Nested pools satisfy C1 ⊂ C5 , so their support
and oracle differences cannot be negative; we report task-bootstrap intervals and one-sided lower
bounds, not sign-test p-values. Provider calls,
rounds, candidate slots, and repeated repairs are
never treated as independent task units.
The experiments proceed in a fixed order. We
first audit canary exposure and score signals with
DeepSeek V4 Flash, then complete a frozen 120instance replication of the canary audit. We repeat the length-term removal on a non-degenerate
DeepSeek cohort and an untouched Qwen3.7-Max
cohort. A zero-call oracle audit measures fixedpool headroom, and a prospective Qwen study expands candidate support with independent restarts.
Protocols were frozen as internal JSON records
before execution; they are not externally timestamped registrations. An appendix endpoint-status
table separates quantities frozen before provider
execution from later secondary analyses; no postoutcome qualification changes a recorded run or
primary endpoint.

The extractor recovers 7/7 deterministic channel
injections and a live canary written into memory
at round 2; these controls show that it detects canaries placed in each declared channel, not that a
round-one canary must reach those channels. State

The score audit separates score movement from
task movement. Most scores of at least 0.8 occur
in failed runs because failures dominate the cohort; relative to the base rate they are only mildly
enriched among successes (Figure 1c), and the runlevel AUC of the maximum score is 0.755 (Appendix H). We do not treat these counts as calibration evidence: only five runs are exact, from three
task instances.
The paired interventions show that the score
responds while exactness does not reliably follow. The two later cohorts are non-degenerate:
each ran only after an eight-task gate, blind to
the length contrast, placed baseline exactness inside a frozen 30–70% window. Removing the
length term sharply reduces mean contribution
score in every study: from 0.429 to 0.069 in the initial DeepSeek pairs, by −0.308 [−0.381, −0.230]
in the non-degenerate DeepSeek replication, and
by −0.117 [−0.150, −0.084] in Qwen, eliminating every score of at least 0.8 in the paired cohorts. Exactness effects in the primary cohorts
are heterogeneous and inconclusive (Table 2; Figure 1b): zero in the initial pairs, whose bootstrap
interval is degenerate (exact bound ±0.31), and
+0.167 and −0.050 in the two non-degenerate
cohorts. The DeepSeek +0.167 estimate is also
within nuisance variation: the three arms of that
replication that share the released score span 5/12
to 7/12 exact, the same two-task spread. A secondary, outcome-aware synthesis of all 42 pairs
gives +0.024 [−0.095, 0.143]. We therefore claim
only that removing the fast-round length term
changes recorded scores without a reliably detectable change in measured exactness, not that
the score is invalid.
Replacing the heuristic outright does not rescue
utility either. Inserting a reference-free executor

into historical-answer retention gave 1/8 versus
0/8 executable finals in a development pilot and
1/20 versus 0/20 in a separately frozen validation
(+0.05 [0, 0.15], one-sided exact p = 0.5), failing
the frozen confirmation rule (Appendix G). The
text-heuristic arm of that validation is the retention
control in Table 3.

Fixed-pool selection has little realized
headroom

318

We execute the final answer and every persisted
round-agent candidate in four independently frozen
original or control cohorts (Table 3). In every cohort, average headroom is between zero and 8.3
points. This is not a confidence statement about
all future tasks: it bounds what any selector could
have recovered from each recorded pool under our
parser, executor, and target-reaching utility.
Conditional on failure, 43 of 46 failed runs
(93%; exact 95% interval [0.82, 0.99]) contain no
parser-accepted executable candidate, and 3 of 46
[0.01, 0.18] are selection failures. As a share of
all 65 eligible runs, the 3 recoverable runs give
the pooled row of Table 3; that interval concerns
similar-task generalization and does not alter the
exact realized-pool bound. Two checks bound
how much this depends on our choices. Including
the seven IMPOSSIBLE-reference runs under reference exactness leaves the same 3 recoverable runs
among 72, and executable validity and reference exactness agree on all 65 eligible final answers. The
parser, however, accepts only 365 of 1,152 candidate slots (32%), and we have not hand-audited the
rejected slots, so a parser false negative would be
counted as a generation failure (Appendix P).
Literal duplication is model-dependent and therefore not the general explanation. Qwen repeats exact text in roughly 81–82% of its 16 slots, whereas
DeepSeek duplicate-text rates are much lower.
Parser-accepted support is small in both model families: across selected arms, 38/72 runs contain no
parseable canonical plan, 22 contain one, eight contain two, and only four contain at least three.

An illustrative fixed-pool selector
calculation

Appendix V gives an illustrative fixed-pool calculation, not a privacy–utility frontier. With binary executor utility on mixed C5 pools (gUsel = ∆u = 1),
the monotone exponential mechanism selects an
executable candidate with mean probability 0.512,
0.575, 0.682, 0.779, and 0.800 at ε = 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8,
respectively, against a non-private offline oracle of
0.80; under the general calibration the same values require ε = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Only the selected
index is covered by the adjacency; candidate texts,
prompts, memories, messages, control flow, and
the executor are trusted (Appendix C).

Related Work and Implications

Test-time optimization uses language models as optimizers or textual-gradient generators (Yang et al.,
2024; Yuksekgonul et al., 2024). Self-consistency,
tree search, and compute-aware inference spend additional test-time computation on diverse samples
or structured exploration (Wang et al., 2023; Yao
et al., 2023a; Zhou et al., 2024; Snell et al., 2025),
and repeated sampling raises coverage whenever
a verifier can recognize a correct sample (Brown
et al., 2024). For agents, Zhu et al. (2025) study parallel sampling, sequential revision, verifiers, and
diversified rollouts. Our nested pools are a narrower realized-support audit inside one runtime,
not a new scaling method; the question they add
is whether the recorded generator exposed any executable alternative before search or selection is
optimized.

Differential privacy calibrates randomized releases to sensitivity (Dwork et al., 2006; Dwork

Candidate selection in test-time LLM agents should
be preceded by an audit of exposure, score consequences, and realized candidate support. In the
audited fixed-topology TacoMAS–PlanCraft configuration, a complete 120-instance canary replication fails to detect post-input exposure and places
a 2.5% one-sided upper bound on the evolve-arm
rate under the audited setting. Removing the dominant score term changes recorded scores without
a reliably detectable change in exactness, and 43
of 46 failed runs lack a parser-accepted executable
candidate, leaving fixed pools only 0 to 8.3 points
of headroom. Additional independent restarts raise
the offline oracle from 0.60 to 0.80 at triple the
token cost. These measurements locate the binding
constraint in generation support for this configuration and expose the limits of the present evidence.

The length-term removal is paired within task

The initial reference-firewall implementation returned online quality zero for all expensive evaluations. The runtime stores a historical best candidate
only when quality strictly improves over an initial
value of zero. Constant-zero quality could therefore prevent candidate persistence independently
of arm assignment. The issue was detected after
the first pair and before interpreting its outcomes.
We froze an amendment that marked both cells
invalid_harness_calibration, excluded them
from every endpoint and the 3M valid-token cap,
retained them in the artifact, and replaced constant
zero with the same reference-free text heuristic in
both arms. The complete pair was then restarted.
The invalid cells used 282,399 tokens; the 20 valid
cells used 2,716,894 tokens and 1,083 calls; total
observed use was 2,999,293 tokens. This near-miss
demonstrates that reference isolation must cover
control flow, not merely omit a reference-derived
metric from the final table.
